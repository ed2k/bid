Known cuebids raw descriptions

1) Michaels cuebid (single-suit opening context)
- A cuebid overcall when opponents have bid only one suit shows a 5-5 two-suiter (or more distributional).
- Over minor opening: shows both majors.
- Over major opening: shows other major plus an unspecified minor.
- Common strength notes in sources: around 8+ to 14+ points depending on system/style.

2) Cuebid response to overcall (quality ask)
- After partner overcalls, a cuebid of opener's suit is forcing and asks overcaller to further describe overcall quality/strength.

3) Western / Jump cuebid
- Jump cuebid asks partner to bid notrump with a stopper in opponents' suit.
- Typically shows a strong hand with a solid or self-sufficient side suit.

4) Control cuebid in slam auctions
- New suit rebid after fit/agreement can be a control cuebid for slam cooperation.
- Often interpreted as showing first- or second-round control and game/slam forcing intent.

5) Cuebid as Stayman substitute after NT interference
- When 1NT systems are disrupted by overcall/interference, cuebid can replace Stayman-like game-forcing inquiry.

6) Cuebid with no stopper opposite opponent suit
- In competitive auctions, cuebid can show values without a good 5-card suit and lacking stopper in opponents' suit.
- Some methods use cuebid instead of 1NT for these no-stopper hands.

7) Strong takeout cuebid
- In higher-level competitive auctions, cuebid can be defined as strong takeout rather than natural.

8) Precision family cuebids
- Alpha cuebid / support-asking style (step responses by controls).
- Beta cuebid / control-asking style.
- Used in strong-club structures with artificial step meanings.

9) Trump cuebid / cue raise
- Competitive cue raise can show support with invitational-or-better values.
- Some notes describe trump cuebids as slam-interest actions after fit is set.

10) Michaels vs weak two in major (GIB note)
- Over weak two in a major, bidding 4 of that same major can show stronger both-minor hand.
- 4NT can show weaker both-minor hand.

11) Re-cuebid continuation
- In two-suiter structures, if responder does not choose one promised suit, advancer may cuebid again to clarify/insist.

12) Drury bid is an artificial bridge convention used by a "passed hand" (a player who previously passed) to invite game after their partner opens the bidding in a major suit ( 1H or 1S)  in the third or fourth seat.
